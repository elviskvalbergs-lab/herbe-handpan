This is the easiest way if you do not know the name of your scale. You play, Oakhum listens.

![The guided round: play the lit note](img:setup-listen)

## Before you start {#before}

- Find a quiet place. A fan, a television or people talking make it harder to hear.
- Put your phone next to the instrument.
- Your browser will ask to use the microphone. Tap Allow.

## Play your notes {#steps}

1. Tap your instrument, then {{setup-listen}}.
2. Say how many notes it has. On a handpan, count the notes on top, the big one in the middle too, then any underneath. If you are not sure, tap {{not-sure-listen}}.
3. Tap Start. The note to play is lit on the picture.
4. Play that note once and let it ring. When Oakhum has heard it, the next one lights up.
5. Go round all your notes. If you play a different note than the lit one, tap the one you are playing instead.
6. When you have played them all, tap {{thats-all}}.

Oakhum then tells you which scale it found. If no known scale has exactly your notes, it keeps the notes just as you played them. You can change any note later.

## It heard the wrong note {#wrong-note}

Play the note again, a little softer, and let it ring. See [It heard the wrong note](/help/it-heard-the-wrong-note) for more tips.

## Is it recording me? {#recording}

The sound is worked out on your phone. Oakhum only listens while this screen is open, and the sound stays on your phone unless you choose to send it to us. See [The microphone and your privacy](/help/microphone-and-privacy).

## I would rather choose the scale {#choose-instead}

At the bottom left there is a button to choose your scale or key instead. You can tap it at any time.

## Find a scale in the list by playing {#in-the-list}

In the list of all scales, {{setup-listen}} does the same: play your notes and Oakhum shows the scales that fit them.
