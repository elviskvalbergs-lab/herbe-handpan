Hearing a real instrument through a phone microphone is hard, and Oakhum does not always get it right. These things help most.

## Quick things to try {#try}

- Go somewhere quiet. Turn off music, a fan or the television.
- Play one note at a time and let it ring.
- Play a little softer. A hard strike on a handpan makes extra sounds.
- Move the phone. Next to the instrument for setting up, a metre or two away for {{listen}}.
- Take the phone out of a thick case if it covers the microphone.

## It names the note one step off {#one-off}

Some instruments are tuned a little higher or lower than usual on purpose. During set-up, Oakhum notices this and tells you. If a single note is named wrongly, you can set it by hand. See [Change the notes of your instrument](/help/change-your-notes#change).

## It hears a higher note than I played {#octave}

Low notes on a handpan or tongue drum ring with higher sounds inside them, and sometimes the microphone picks those up instead. Play the note softly, near the middle of the note, and let it ring.

## During set-up it skips or repeats a note {#setup}

If you play a different note than the lit one, tap the one you are playing on the picture. If it will not hear a note at all, use the button at the bottom left to choose your scale from the list instead.

## Send us the recording {#report}

This is the best way to get it fixed.

1. Where Oakhum listened, tap {{send-recording}}.
2. Write what was wrong. For example: it did not hear my lowest note.
3. Leave the recording switched on if you can. You can listen to it first.
4. Add a photo of the instrument and your email if you like. With your email we tell you when it is fixed.
5. Tap Send.

## The fingering or the tuning itself is wrong {#data}

If a chart or scale in Oakhum does not match your instrument, tell us with {{feedback}} in {{settings}}. Say which instrument it is and who made it.
