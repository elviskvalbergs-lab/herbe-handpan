## Note {#note}

One sound of your instrument: one dent on a handpan, one tongue on a tongue drum, one key on a kalimba, one fingering on a flute or a recorder. Notes have letter names from A to G.

## Sharp and flat {#sharp-flat}

The notes between the letters. C# is said C sharp and is a little higher than C. D♭ is said D flat and is a little lower than D. C# and D♭ are the same note written two ways. You can choose which way Oakhum writes them in the settings.

## The number after a note {#octave}

A4 and A3 are both the note A. The number says how high it is: a bigger number is higher. The distance from A3 to A4 is called an octave.

## Ding {#ding}

The big note in the middle of a handpan. It is usually the lowest note and gives the scale its first letter: a D Kurd has a D ding.

## Shell {#shell}

A handpan is two steel bowls joined together. The top shell has the ding and the notes around it. Some handpans have extra notes on the bottom shell.

## Scale {#scale}

The set of notes your instrument has. A handpan, tongue drum or kalimba is built in one scale and cannot play notes outside it. The scale has a name, such as D Kurd or C major.

## Tuning {#tuning}

On Oakhum, another word for the scale an instrument is built in.

## Key {#key}

The note that a scale or a song feels at home on. A kalimba in C, an ocarina in G. A song in the key of D minor ends comfortably on D.

## Major and minor {#major-minor}

Two colours of sound. Major sounds bright and open. Minor sounds darker or sadder. Neither is harder to play.

## Chord {#chord}

Two or more notes that sound good together. On a handpan you strike them together or one after another.

## Chord names {#chord-names}

A chord is named after its main note. D Minor is a minor chord built on D, and is written Dm for short. A letter alone, such as F, means F Major.

## Root {#root}

The main note of a chord: the D in D Minor. Playing only the roots is the simplest way to follow a song.

## Arpeggio {#arpeggio}

The notes of a chord played one after another. An ocarina, a flute or a recorder plays one note at a time, so it always plays chords this way.

## Progression {#progression}

Chords in an order, played round and round. Most songs are built on one. In Oakhum you keep one as a playlist. See [Playlists](/help/playlists).

## Bar and beat {#bar}

The beat is the steady count you tap your foot to. A bar is a small group of beats, most often four. One, two, three, four, and round again.

## Tempo and BPM {#tempo}

How fast the beat goes. BPM means beats per minute. 60 is one beat a second. 80 is calm, 120 is lively.

## Fingering {#fingering}

Which holes to cover on an ocarina, a flute or a recorder to play one note.

## Tab {#tab}

A simple way to write a tune for one kind of instrument: numbers for a handpan or kalimba, small fingering pictures for a flute, ocarina or recorder.

## In tune {#in-tune}

A note is in tune when it sounds at the pitch it should. A little low is called flat, a little high is called sharp.
