Most songs are a few chords going round and round. A playlist keeps such a row of chords: in order, and the same chord can come back as often as you like.

![The Playlists tab](img:playlists)

## Start a playlist {#start}

There are four ways.

1. The quickest: on the {{chords}} tab, tap a chord, then its three dots, then {{ready-progressions}}, then Save on one you like.
2. Tap the bookmark on the chosen chord ({{add-to-playlist}}), then {{new-playlist}}. Later, the bookmark adds more chords to the end of it.
3. On the {{playlists}} tab, tap {{new-playlist}}.
4. Paste the chords of a song. See [Paste a chord sheet](/help/chord-sheets).

The first time, Oakhum asks for your email, because playlists are kept in your free account. See [Saving and accounts](/help/accounts-and-saving). The one exception is a pasted chord sheet: you can play it straight away, and it is gone when you close the page unless you save it.

## Play along {#play}

Open the playlist and tap Play. Oakhum plays the chords in order, in time, and lights the notes on your instrument. Tap a chord in the list to hear just that one. The button next to Play goes back to the first chord.

On an ocarina, flute or recorder Play shows the fingering of each note in turn, in time. To go at your own pace, open the playlist and tap {{practice}}: it takes you through the fingerings of each chord, note by note.

![A playlist with four chords](img:playlist)

## Four ways to play it {#levels}

On a handpan, tongue drum or kalimba you choose how much Oakhum shows you, above the Play button.

- {{level-chord}}: the whole chord at once.
- {{level-roots}}: only the main note of each chord. A good place to start.
- {{level-arpeggio}}: the notes of the chord one after another.
- {{level-groove}}: a rhythm with left and right hands.

## Tempo and bars {#tempo}

- {{tempo}} is how fast it goes, in beats per minute. 80 is calm. Use the plus and minus buttons.
- Beats per bar sets the count, for example 4 for most songs and 3 for a waltz.
- On each chord, the plus and minus buttons say how many bars it lasts.

## Change the chords {#edit}

The button at the end of each row lets you duplicate, move or remove that chord. Tap {{order}} to rearrange several. The three dots at the top rename or delete the playlist.

## Use a playlist on another instrument {#other}

A playlist plays on any of your instruments that has the chords. Playlists with chords that this instrument does not have are tucked away at the top of the list. Tap that line to show them. They still play, with the chords it does have.

## The list is empty {#empty}

You have no playlists yet. Tap the bookmark on any chord on the {{chords}} tab, or tap {{chord-sheet}}.
