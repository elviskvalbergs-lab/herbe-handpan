For an instrument with finger holes, Oakhum needs two things: the kind of instrument, and its key.

## Ocarina {#ocarina}

1. On the first screen, tap Ocarina.
2. Choose how many holes it has. A 12 hole ocarina is the most common one.
3. Choose its key. It is usually written on the ocarina or its box. C is the most common.
4. If there are several sizes in that key, choose yours by its lowest note.

Some ocarinas, such as pendants with 7 or 8 holes, have no published chart yet. Oakhum sets these up note by note with the tuner: you blow each fingering and it fills in the note. Double and triple ocarinas are not in Oakhum yet; you can ask for yours.

## Flute or whistle {#flute}

1. On the first screen, tap Flute or whistle.
2. Choose your flute: a tin whistle in D or C, or a Native American flute with 5 or 6 holes.
3. For a Native American flute, choose its key.

A whistle in another key, a bansuri, a fife or a flute made by hand is set up note by note with the tuner.

## Recorder {#recorder}

1. On the first screen, tap Recorder.
2. Choose the fingering: Baroque, also called English, is on most recorders. German fingering is on some school recorders. The way to tell: on a German one, F is played by lifting the next finger after E.
3. Choose its size or key.

## I do not know the key {#listen}

Tap {{not-sure-listen}}, or the button to play it on the key step. Play a few notes and Oakhum works it out.

## Check it by playing {#check}

On the last step you can tap {{check-playing}}. Oakhum shows each fingering and listens while you blow it, so you know the chart fits your instrument.

## Where do the fingerings come from? {#source}

Every fingering chart in Oakhum comes from a chart a maker or publisher has printed. If your instrument plays differently, tell us with {{feedback}} in {{settings}}.
