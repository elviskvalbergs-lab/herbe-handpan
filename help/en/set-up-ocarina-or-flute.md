For an ocarina, a flute or a recorder, Oakhum needs two things: the kind of instrument, and its key.

## Ocarina {#ocarina}

1. On the first screen, tap Ocarina.
2. Choose how many holes it has. A 12 hole ocarina is the most common one.
3. Choose its key. It is usually written on the ocarina or its box. C is the most common.
4. If there are several sizes in that key, choose yours by its lowest note.

For some ocarinas, such as small pendants with 4, 7 or 8 holes, there is no ready chart. Oakhum sets these up note by note with the tuner: you blow each fingering and it fills in the note.

## Flute or whistle {#flute}

1. On the first screen, tap Flute or whistle.
2. Choose your flute: a tin whistle, or a Native American flute with 5 or 6 holes.
3. Choose its key. A tin whistle is usually in D.

Another kind of flute, such as a bansuri or one made by hand, is set up note by note with the tuner.

## Recorder {#recorder}

1. On the first screen, tap Recorder.
2. Choose its fingering. Most recorders have Baroque fingering, also called English. Some school sopranos have German fingering.
3. Choose its key: C for a soprano or tenor, F for an alto.

Another recorder, such as a bass, is set up note by note with the tuner.

## I do not know the key {#listen}

Tap {{not-sure-listen}} on the first step, or {{dont-know-play}} on the key step. Play a few notes and Oakhum works it out.

## Check it by playing {#check}

On the last step you can tap {{check-playing}}. Oakhum shows each fingering and listens while you blow it, so you know the chart fits your instrument.

## Where do the fingerings come from? {#source}

Every fingering chart in Oakhum comes from a published chart by a maker. If your instrument plays differently, tell us with {{feedback}} in {{settings}}.
