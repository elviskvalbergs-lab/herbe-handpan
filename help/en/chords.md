{{chords}} is the first tab of every instrument. It only shows chords that your instrument really has the notes for.

![The Chords tab with D Minor chosen](img:chords)

## Play a chord {#play}

1. Open the {{chords}} tab. Under the picture, {{chords-all}} is chosen.
2. Tap a chord in the list. Its notes light up on the picture and you hear it.
3. Play the lit notes on your own instrument.
4. Tap the {{level-chord}} button to hear it again.

On an ocarina, flute or recorder you can only play one note at a time. So Oakhum plays the notes of the chord one after another, and shows the fingering for each. Tap 1, 2 or 3 to see each fingering.

## Why does it only show a few chords? {#main-chords}

Oakhum starts with the main chords: the ones that fit your scale best. Above the list there is a line with the number of chords. Tap {{show-all}} there to see every chord your notes can make.

## Find a certain chord {#filters}

Tap {{chords-filters}} to choose by the first note of the chord, the number of notes, or the kind of chord, such as Major or Minor.

## Keep a chord {#keep}

Tap the bookmark next to the chosen chord to add it to a playlist. Chords that are in a playlist show a small bookmark in the list. See [Playlists](/help/playlists).

## More about one chord {#more}

Tap the three dots on the chosen chord. On a handpan, tongue drum or kalimba you can {{play-scale}}, copy a link to the chord, find progressions with it, and see chords that share notes with it. On an ocarina, flute or recorder you can move a note to another octave.

## The other two buttons under the picture {#modes}

- {{chords-identify}}: tap notes on the picture and Oakhum names the chord. See [Find out which chord you are playing](/help/identify-a-chord).
- {{listen}}: Oakhum listens to music nearby and shows the chords. See [Listen](/help/listen).

## What is a chord? {#what}

A few notes that sound good together. See [Words explained](/help/glossary#chord).
