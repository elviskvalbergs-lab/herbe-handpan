{{songs}} is the second tab of every instrument. It only lists songs your instrument can play.

![A song on a handpan: the next note is lit](img:song)

## Play a song {#steps}

1. Open the {{songs}} tab.
2. Choose a list: the songs of the season, {{songs-popular}}, or {{songs-countries}} to see songs from one country.
3. Tap a song. The first note is lit on the picture. Play it, then tap the arrow for the next note.
4. Or tap Play and Oakhum plays the melody while the notes light up. Use the 1× button to slow it down.
5. The button for {{back-to-start}} takes you to the first note again.

## Let it wait for you {#wait}

Tap {{song-wait}}. Oakhum listens, and the song moves on each time it hears you play the lit note. Take as long as you need. Tap it again to stop. Your browser asks to use the microphone the first time.

## Practise one part {#loop}

1. Go to the first note of the part.
2. Tap {{song-loop}}.
3. Go to the last note of the part and tap the button again. It now says Loop to here.
4. Play goes round that part until you tap it once more.

## Tab and Notes {#views}

Below the picture you can choose how the melody is written.

- {{tab-view}} is written the way players of your instrument read it. On a handpan the notes are numbered, with R and L for the right and left hand. On a kalimba it is the usual number notation. On an ocarina, flute or recorder it is a small fingering picture for each note.
- {{notes-view}} shows the note names.

Some songs also have their words. There is a button to {{print-tab}}.

## Songs that almost fit {#almost}

Under {{almost-fit}} are songs that need one note your instrument does not have. That note is marked in the song and never swapped for another: leave it out, or sing it.

## The song moved to a different key {#key}

Oakhum moves each song to a key that fits your instrument. So the same song can be in C major on one instrument and A major on another. The melody is the same.

## Where do the songs come from? {#source}

The tunes are traditional. Each melody was read from a published score: Wikipedia, or a songbook that is out of copyright. The source is shown under each song.

## My song is not there {#request}

Ask for it with {{feedback}} in {{settings}}. For now the list is folk, children's and Christmas songs. If you know a song's chords, you can also play along with them: see [Paste a chord sheet](/help/chord-sheets).
